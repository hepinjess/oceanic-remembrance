The invitation

Remembering our body’s ancient fluency with water, and the oceanic origins we carry within.

Through freediving, water dance, and somatic movement, we return to a softer, more fluid body-mind, settling the nervous system and rewilding our ways of being. Alongside the migrating lives of the sea, we enter their unique sensory worlds — following their orientation, rhythms, and ways of becoming, while remembering the resilience held within our own bodies.

*All levels welcome

The largest gathering of mobula rays on Earth

They arrive in their thousands.

Why Baja, why these dates

Where desert meets sea.

We meet at a special threshold, in a special time of year. Every spring, Mobula rays gather in La Ventana, moving through the bay in great winged formations as they journey between worlds.

They are joined by sea lions, whales, dolphins, and on the lucky days, orcas. For a few weeks, the bay becomes a meeting place for residents and nomads. 

On land, the desert holds its own rhythm: cardón cacti, dry riverbeds, and long empty beaches.

We arrive with gratitude, entering an ecosystem alive with wisdom and generosity, allowing the place itself to slow us down, soften us, and open our senses.

Interspecies learning

Learning alongside
What if an interspecies encounter doesn’t begin with chasing?

We are redefining what it means to meet ocean wildlife. To make space, rather than chase, is our devotion to the sea — working to steward a new type of ecotourism.

To make space is to invite the encounter within. We weave therapeutic water arts, somatics, marine science, and somatic freediving into time spent deeply in the water — practicing, exploring, and becoming attuned to the place rather than searching for the next sighting.

In this season of abundance, the bay is already full of life. We slow down enough to let encounters unfold, discovering what becomes possible when we stop chasing the moment.

Engine off

Our captain, a former fisherman, takes us away from the other boats and cuts the motor. Then we drift.

No chasing

We slip into the water quietly and keep our distance from the school.

Their call

Four pillars of practice

A weaving field of practice.

01

Water Dance & Aquatic Bodywork

Meditation, intuitive movement and contact improvisation, in shallow and deep water. We follow the spirals found in nature and in our own bodies, waking the amphibious body and its fluid intelligence, and learning to rest within ourselves.

02

Somatic freediving training & ocean safari boat trips

For beginners and experienced divers alike. Breathwork, practical guidance and a somatic approach, with no focus on numbers. Each day we take the boat out to explore different waters and quiet corners of the bay, letting each progression arrive in its own time.

03

Embodied rituals

Ecosomatics in the cactus forest and dry arroyos. Time in silence, dancing on empty beaches, a bonfire at night. These are ways to integrate what the water gives, and to celebrate our creative harvest.

04

Companionship

Sharing and writing circles, slow meals, long talks under desert stars. A small circle meeting in honest conversation, the kind where friendship quietly begins and stays.

IN THEIR WORDS

From past seasons

“I arrived hoping to find something I needed for myself, and left feeling connected to so much more.”

— Past participant, 2026

This retreat came into my life at exactly the moment I needed it. What Julia, Bethany, and Jess created was something really special: a space that invited us to turn inward while also discovering the beauty and depth of being in community with one another. Through freediving, meditation, somatic practices in the water, movement, dance, and conversation, I found new ways to connect with myself and the particular season of life I was moving through. But what I hadn’t anticipated was just how much of that experience would be shaped by the people around me. There was a tenderness and openness in the group that allowed me to feel genuinely held, while still having the freedom to explore my own experience. The relationships that grew out of those days were some of the most unexpected and meaningful gifts of the retreat.

Something I deeply admired was Julia’s relationship with the place itself. I’ve been on beautiful retreats that, in many ways, could have happened anywhere. This was something entirely different. The experience was woven into the landscape, history, culture, and community of La Ventana and Baja California Sur. There was a mindfulness about where we were, whose home we were visiting, and the relationships that made our time there possible. You could feel that care in the local partnerships Julia has cultivated, in the way she approaches her work, and in the generosity with which she welcomed us into a place she so clearly loves. There was a sense of reciprocity that ran through the entire experience, a feeling that we weren’t simply arriving somewhere to receive something for ourselves, but were being invited into a much larger web of relationships and care. I think that is part of what made the retreat feel so loving and alive.

And of course, there was the freediving! Julia and her team are incredibly skilled freedivers and thoughtful teachers. I grew in my abilities in ways I’m genuinely proud of, but some of the most meaningful learning happened in my relationship with discomfort. I found myself meeting moments of stress and uncertainty in the water with a little more ease, a little more curiosity, and a lot more grace. The environment they created made it possible to explore my edges without feeling pushed beyond them. There is something powerful about discovering what you’re capable of when you feel so supported in the trying.

I left with stronger freediving skills, a deeper connection to myself, unexpected friendships, and a sense of gratitude that has stayed with me. What Julia and her team have created is so much more than a collection of beautiful experiences. There is real thought, integrity, generosity, and love woven into the way it all comes together. I arrived hoping to find something I needed for myself, and left feeling connected to so much more.

“It realigned my North Star.”

— Lara · Remembrance Participant 2026

The retreat with Jess, Beth, and Julia was a truly awakening experience for me. I hadn’t realized how much I had closed myself off from connection through a busy life, demanding work, and everyday routine. Being there helped me rediscover genuine, authentic connection and reminded me of the beauty in people and the world around us.

What I thought would simply be about learning to freedive and swim with megafauna (which I did) became something much deeper. It realigned my North Star and brought me back to what I need to feel happiest and most authentic.

The team

Three facilitators, between Baja and Bali.

Julia

Founder, Soul & Sea Somatics

A Baja-based deep water dancer, freediving instructor and breathwork facilitator with Colombian roots.

Read Julia's story +

Julia is a Baja-based Deep Water Dancer, certified freediving instructor, and breathwork facilitator with Colombian roots.

She guides people into the water with calm clarity and a touch of playfulness, inviting softness, trust in the breath, and ease in movement.

Beyond her ocean projects, she also co-leads expeditions to Mongolia to help bring clean drinking water to rural communities. Her life's work is a form of activism, supporting equal rights for humans, the Earth, and the more-than-human world.

Bethany

Dancer & aquatic bodyworker

A Bali-based dancer and freediver, with roots in artistic swimming and a decade of dance and contemporary art in Indonesia.

Read Bethany's story +

Bethany is a trained dancer, freediver, and aquatic bodyworker based in Bali, Indonesia. With roots in artistic swimming and a decade studying dance and contemporary art in Indonesia, she works at the intersection of collective practice and expressive arts.

She supports residency and retreat projects, and guides individuals in unwinding and reconnecting through womb-like water immersion, somatic movement, and improvisation, cultivating rituals of collective care and co-creative, generative futures.

Jess Hepin

Freediving instructor & artist

A Molchanovs freediving instructor and water, dance and sound artist from Taiwan, based in Bali.

Read Jess's story +

Jess is an experienced freediving instructor and water, dance and sound artist from Taiwan. Her work is rooted in the embodiment of Eastern philosophy and deep ecology.

She co-created several programs within the Subtle Waters project, supporting the emergence of creative expression through shared practice, and ripples of change from personal healing toward a world we hope to inhabit.

She continues to meet depth with tenderness, gently restoring intimacy between the human body, psyche, and the living sea.

Questions

Do I need prior experience?

No experience is required. Being comfortable swimming is enough. You’ll learn theory, equalization, and breath-hold techniques step by step. Certified divers are welcome to refine their skills and explore new depths.

What if I feel anxious about the ocean?

That’s natural. We approach the water gradually, building trust through breathwork, clear guidance, and supportive group dynamics. Baja’s waters are typically warm and calm, making it an ideal place to learn.

What are the temperatures like?

Air temperatures are usually in the high 80s to low 90s °F. Water temperatures are warm — often in the 80s °F. Expect mostly sunny days, with occasional moderate wind.

Where do I fly into?

Fly into Los Cabos (SJD) — about a 2.5-hour drive — or La Paz (LAP) — about 45 minutes away.

Can you accommodate dietary needs?

Yes. We collect dietary information in advance and provide nourishing options for different eating styles and allergies.
