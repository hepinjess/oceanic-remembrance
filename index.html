The invitation

A homecoming to our oceanic origins, to the soft, liquid body-mind and the resilience it grows.

Through freediving, water dance and somatic movement, we explore our bonds with the season’s migrating sea animals and rewild into a deeper embodied intelligence.

Oceanic Remembrance is an invitation to sense their presence through the body, and to turn that connection into care and protection for the sea and all its life.

The largest gathering of mobula rays on Earth

They arrive in their thousands.

Why Baja, why these dates

Where desert meets sea.

Every spring, Munk's devil rays come to the bay of La Ventana to feed, court and mate. Our dates fall at the height of the season, when the water is usually calm and clear.

On land, it's desert: tall cardón cacti, dry riverbeds, long empty beaches. It's a quiet place, and most people slow down within a day or two.

Interspecies learning

Learning alongside
whales, mobula rays, sea lions…

The bay is shared water, and we come as guests of the tide, bodies in ecstatic quiet, senses open.

Each day in the water deepens our oceanic kinship. We freedive and dance among them, holding a space that feels safe and spacious for humans and animals alike, and let their rhythm teach ours. The aim is symbiosis, not spectacle. Day by day, we become a little more like them.

Engine off

Our captain, a former fisherman, takes us away from the other boats and cuts the motor. Then we drift.

No chasing

We slip into the water quietly and keep our distance from the school.

Their call

The rays decide how close they come. Some days that's very close.

It's peak season, so we'll very likely see them. We just can't put it on the schedule.

Four pillars of practice

A weaving field of practice.

01

Water dance

Meditation, intuitive movement and contact improvisation, in shallow and deep water. We follow the spirals found in nature and in our own bodies, waking the amphibious body and its fluid intelligence, and learning to rest within ourselves.

02

Somatic freediving & ocean safari

For beginners and experienced divers alike. Breathwork, practical guidance and a somatic approach, with no focus on numbers. Each day we take the boat out to explore different waters and quiet corners of the bay, letting each progression arrive in its own time.

03

Embodied rituals

Ecosomatics in the cactus forest and dry arroyos. Time in silence, dancing on empty beaches, a bonfire at night. These are ways to integrate what the water gives, and to celebrate our creative harvest.

04

Companionship

Sharing and writing circles, slow meals, long talks under desert stars. A small circle meeting in honest conversation, the kind where friendship quietly begins and stays.

Questions

Do I need dance or diving experience?

No. If you're comfortable swimming, that's enough. We teach everything step by step.

What if I feel anxious in the ocean?

That's normal, and you're not alone. We go slowly, start in shallow water, and you can always sit a session out.

Where do I fly into?

La Paz (LAP), about 45 minutes away, or Los Cabos (SJD), about 2.5 hours.

Can you accommodate dietary needs?

Yes. We ask when you register and cook for different eating styles and allergies.
